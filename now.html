Now | September 2026

What has my
attention.

There is a particular kind of focus that comes in September, at least for me. The summer was long and full, and now the calendar is clearing in the way that only happens when you stop adding things to it. I am using the space intentionally. Not resting exactly, but consolidating, which feels different and probably is.

In progress.

Thinking carefully about Q4 on the IT side. Forty-plus clients means forty-plus end-of-year conversations about budgets, renewals, and what they actually need versus what they think they need. I am trying to get ahead of those conversations rather than reactive to them, which requires knowing my own priorities clearly enough to hold them while someone else is in the room with a different set.

The KDP book is out and I am watching what it does. Sales data is only part of it. I am more interested in whether the framing landed, whether the audience I was writing for recognizes themselves in it. That feedback takes longer to arrive and you have to be patient enough to wait for it rather than inventing conclusions from incomplete numbers.

Paying attention to.

South Texas water supply. The Nueces River basin has been under pressure for a while and the conversation about it is mostly happening in rooms most people are not in. I keep reading the reporting, the water authority documents, the agricultural studies. It is the kind of infrastructure issue that does not feel urgent until it is, and then it is too late to feel anything but caught off guard.

How AI is actually landing in small and mid-size businesses, not the enterprise case studies, but the plumber, the insurance agency, the regional distributor. The gap between what the tools can do and what people are willing to learn to use is where the real story is. I see it constantly with clients. The friction is almost never technical.
