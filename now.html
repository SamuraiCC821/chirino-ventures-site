Now | September 2026

What has my
attention.

September on the Gulf Coast means the heat is finally negotiating. It is still there, still heavy, but there is a different quality to the mornings now, a reminder that seasons do shift even here. Peak hurricane season is also peak awareness, the kind of ambient alertness that living on the water builds into you over time. I am moving into fall with more clarity than I had in the spring, which is not nothing.

In progress.

Book 1 of the KDP series is published. That sentence still feels slightly surreal to write. The immediate work now is less about the book and more about the discipline of not immediately jumping to Book 2 before I understand what Book 1 is actually doing in the market. Sitting with the data for a minute. Letting it tell me something before I react.

On the client side, I have been refining which conversations I want to be in. Forty-plus clients across South Texas is a broad surface, and not every relationship is the same quality. I am thinking carefully about where my attention produces the most durable value, for them and for the business, and making some quiet decisions accordingly.

Paying attention to.

Hurricane preparedness culture, specifically why it is still so uneven. Some people on this coast are genuinely ready. Others, including long-timers, are not, and the gap is not about knowledge. It is about risk tolerance, inertia, and what people allow themselves to imagine happening. Interesting to watch from the inside of a community that has been through it before.

The Port of Corpus Christi and what its growth actually means for South Texas long term. There is a version of this story that is straightforwardly good for the region and a version that creates pressures the infrastructure and the environment are not ready for. The honest answer is probably both, which is why I keep reading about it.
